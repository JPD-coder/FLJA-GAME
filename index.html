The basics

  • Singles or doubles. Pick the match type above the teams. Doubles is two on two, with a front-court and a back-court player on each team. Singles is one on one: you cover the whole court alone, and you can add a photo so your face is on your player.
  • Music. On the hosted game a ♪ Music button opens the playlist for the view you are in. While it is open the ball and crowd sounds stand down so the music is clear; close the player and they come back.
  • Who you play. Under each team, Played by has a switch for the front-court player and one for the back-court player: set each to Player 1 or CPU. Set both to Player 1 and the game hands you whichever player can reach the ball. Set one to CPU and you stay on that position while the CPU plays your partner.
  • Five views. Choose a View on the setup screen: Standard, Aerial (a raised, angled camera behind your players), 90s Throwback (the first fronton and hand-drawn players), 80s Retro (an arcade-cabinet look in chunky pixels), or 50s Vintage (black-and-white blocks on a tube television). The View button changes view during a match without interrupting it. In the Aerial view you also move across the court: up and down run toward the front and back walls, left and right step toward the side wall or the open side. You have to be across to the ball to catch it, and holding left or right as you throw sends the ball to that side. A Cortada or Costado comes off the side wall and back across the court. The rules are the same in every view: the wall is good up to its gold line.
  • Run and catch. Use the arrow buttons, or hold a finger on the court to run there. Any ball that passes one of your players is caught, from the floor up to a full stretch overhead, on the fly or after one bounce. Only a ball well over his head goes by.
  • Shots. Saque (the serve) starts the point. In play, Pelotazo (white path) is the normal throw and there are three power moves, each with its own colored path: Rebota (green) carries to the back wall, Cortada (pink) cuts off the side wall and drops sharply by the front wall, Costado (aqua) is a backhand off the side wall that comes back deep and low. A red path is a fault.
  • Score. Every throw must hit the front wall above the low red line, and the wall is good all the way up to the gold line at its top. A ball that goes over the top of the front or back wall is out. Miss, or let the ball bounce twice, and the other team scores. Serves must land between lines 4 and 7.
  • Rebote clock. When the pelota comes off the back wall and one of your players catches it, front court or back court, it must go back to the front wall in one motion. You have three seconds on Amateur pace, two on Standard and one on Pro to press a shot button. Hold it longer and the ball drops to the floor: point to the other team.

Your five buttons

Saque
The serve. It starts the point and must land between lines 4 and 7. This button only lights up on a serve.
Pelotazo
Your standard throw to the front wall. The safe way to keep the ball in play.
Rebota
Power move. The big throw. It comes off the front wall hard enough to carry to the back wall.
Cortada
Power move. A shoulder-level cut shot that touches the side wall up by lines 1 to 3 before the front wall, then drops sharply near the front.
Costado
Power move. A stylish, difficult backhand played off the side wall up by lines 1 to 3 before the front wall. It comes back deep and low.

Calls you will see on court

Rebote
Tracking the ball past you, taking the carom off the back wall and firing it straight back to the front wall in one fluid motion. The back wall lights up when the pelota strikes it, and whoever catches it has three seconds (Amateur), two (Standard) or one (Pro) to release. Hold longer and the ball drops: point to the other team.
Rabo
A ball taken directly off the back wall on the fly: a lightning catch and release with no time to cradle it.
Chic-Chac
The ball strikes the floor just before the back wall, a rapid double bounce that comes off fast and low.
Chula
The perfect shot. The ball lands in the seam where the floor meets the back wall and rolls out instead of bouncing. No return is possible, so it wins the point.

More fronton talk

Lanzar / Lanzamiento
To throw, and a throw.
Remate
An attacking throw meant to win the point, such as a Cortada or a Costado.
Frontis
The front wall. Every throw must hit it between the lines.
Cesta
The curved basket used to catch and throw the ball.
